# Six words named two subjects, and the schema subject now owns the word

**R13 of `FINDINGS-126.md`, closed.** The owner's ruling of 2026-11-18 is the whole
specification:

> "As for the six words, it has to be schema. OpenAPI-derived thing should not be even
> considered for the class emission as far as I understand."

## THE RULE THIS CASE NEEDS, IN ONE SENTENCE

`docs/plan/rebuild-plan.md` §1 says **one import path per class** — it forbids one CLASS
reached at two paths and says nothing about one WORD standing for two subjects, which is why
this went uncaught. The converse clause is:

> **Where two subjects answer to one word, the SCHEMA subject owns the short flat name and
> the OpenAPI-derived one is reachable only at its own module path.**

Stated in `src/adcp/types/__init__.py`'s own docstring and in
`adcp.types._restored.VENDORED`, so the next reader meets it where the collision is decided
rather than here.

## WHERE THE REGISTRY NAMES ENTERED THE NAMESPACE — TWO LAYERS, NOT ONE

The flat facade is a chain of consulted layers (`adcp/types/__init__.py`'s `__getattr__`).
`adcp.types.registry` — generated from an **OpenAPI** spec by `make regenerate-registry`,
so vendored rather than compiled — reached that chain at **two** of them:

| layer | how registry's words got in | words |
|---|---|---|
| `adcp.types._restored` (5th consult) | `registry` is an entry of `MODULES`, and `_surface()` offered every word it declares except those `compat` or `_adapter` bind | 151 |
| `adcp.types.legacy_names` (7th consult) | generated by `survey/emit_legacy_names.py`; 148 of its rows are `from adcp.types.registry import <word>` | 148 |

**So an ordering fix could not have worked, and the measurement says by how much.** Of the
ten words with two subjects, three are decided before `legacy_names` is reached and seven
only at it: moving one consult ahead of another closes three and leaves seven, and the next
layer added re-opens whichever three it closed.

## THE FIX

**One screen, said at every layer** — `adcp.types._decided` in
`src/adcp/types/__init__.py`. Every layer's answer passes through it, and an answer whose
class was DEFINED in `adcp.types.registry` is replaced by the subject a DOMAIN path gives
that word, where one does. Two properties follow from screening what a layer *answers*
rather than reordering the layers:

* it covers both entry points and every layer added later;
* it can only ever *redirect a contested word*, never add or remove a flat name.

`_domain_subjects` is the subject lookup, and it reads a domain module the two ways a domain
module publishes: `adcp.types._surface._flat.__all__` (the compiler's cross-domain
consolidation of what the 23 domains ADVERTISE — which already declines a word two domains
publish) and, for a word a domain re-exports without advertising, the domain module's own
namespace. The second reading is what reaches the seven `adcp.core` words: upstream's 444
lines of hand-written SDK types are restored at `adcp.core`, re-exported at
`adcp.types.core` in the `X as X` form, and deliberately kept out of that module's `__all__`
because `__all__` there is the compiler's export set. A reading of `_flat` alone answers
three of the ten.

**And the advertisement moved with the binding**, in `src/adcp/types/_restored.py`. That
module's `_surface()` already subtracts a word `compat` or `_adapter` binds — "precedence
said in the `__all__` and not only in the lookup" — and the same subtraction now reaches a
vendored word a domain path owns, through the same `_domain_subjects`, so one question is
answered in one place. Without it `_restored.__all__` would offer nine words the namespace
no longer answers from it, and
`tests_emit/test_the_restored_flat_surface.py::test_every_name_the_surface_offers_resolves_flat_and_is_one_object`
would have reddened — the gate that asserts exactly this agreement. Registry's offer goes
**151 → 142**; `registry.py` is untouched, and nothing is deleted or shrunk.

`STATED` is excluded from the subtraction for its own reason and not for this one: upstream's
own flat word is upstream's statement to make. Of its eight words only `BrandSource` is
registry's, no domain path carries that word, so the clause decides nothing today and is
written for the word that moves later.

`isinstance` holds for a value built from the document's own class:
`isinstance(PolicySummary.model_validate(payload), adcp.types.PolicySummary)` is **True**
and against `adcp.types.registry.PolicySummary` is **False**. `adcp.Policy` and the other
root spellings were already the schema subject's and did not move.

Unmoved and checked, because each is a word the screen could have taken and must not:
`BrandSource` (stated by upstream from `registry`), `AuthorizedAgent` (registry's, and the
two-subject question for it is `FINDINGS-rootnames.md`'s), `RegistryBaseModel` (`registry`
imports it from `adcp.types.base`, so it was never the vendored module's subject — the screen
asks where a class was DEFINED, not which layer handed it over), `TaskStatus` (ours, from
`enums/task-status.json`), `Protocol` (still an `AttributeError`; the screen adds no binding).

## `__all__` AND `__dir__`

**They agree with the new binding, and the gap they still have is pre-existing and wider than
these words.** `adcp.types.__all__` is the union of four layers' advertisements (`compat`,
`compat.__adcp_flat_only__`, `_adapter`, `_restored`, `_flat`); `legacy_names` and `aliases`
are not in it at all, and neither is `adcp.core`.

* Before: `Policy` was advertised — by `_restored`, as registry's — and bound to registry.
  The facade agreed with itself about the wrong subject.
* After: the word is bound to the documented subject and is advertised by nobody, which is
  the state the other eleven `adcp.core` words were already in. Measured: of the 21 words
  `adcp.types.core` re-exports from `adcp.core`, **19 resolve flat, 0 are advertised**
  (`Protocol` and `TaskStatus` are the two that do not resolve to `adcp.core`'s class, the
  second deliberately).
* `__dir__` unions the same five lists, so it moved exactly as `__all__` did.

So no word is advertised with one subject and bound to another. **What is still open is a
surface question for the owner, not a defect of this change**: should the flat surface
advertise the words it serves from `adcp.core` and `legacy_names`? A blanket union is the
wrong fix — it would advertise `Protocol`, which resolves to nothing, and
`tests/test_public_words_resolve.py` grades an advertised word that does not resolve as an
outage.

## OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE OWNER

1. **`Metric` is the one contested word this change does not move.** Its rival subject is
   `adcp.types.capabilities.Metric`, which is the *disambiguation re-export layer*
   (CLAUDE.md's import architecture) and not a domain path: the class it points at is
   `adcp.types.protocol.GetAdCPCapabilitiesResponseMeasurementMetricsItem`, and no document
   gives it the word `Metric`. The ruling as implemented asks "does a DOMAIN path own this
   word", so the vendored `Metric` keeps the flat spelling. If the ruling extends to a word a
   re-export layer gives a schema class, that is a one-line change to `_domain_subjects` —
   but it is the owner's call, because it would make an alias layer's word beat a vendored
   class's own.
2. **Whether the 142 uncontested registry words belong on the flat surface at all** is still
   the open question `adcp.types._restored`'s docstring records, and this ruling does not
   settle it: "which subject owns a contested word" and "should an uncontested vendored word
   be flat" are two questions, and only the first has an answer.
3. **`survey/emit_legacy_names.py` resolved these words to the vendored subject**, and its
   output is where 148 of registry's words reach the flat surface. The facade screen makes
   that harmless, but the generator is still deciding a word by a rule the ruling has now
   overridden; re-running it will keep reproducing the rows. That is a different lane
   (generated file, out of this scope) and is named here so it is not rediscovered.
