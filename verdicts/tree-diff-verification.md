something to a consumer is the **importable name**, so the census below is import
resolution in each tree's own interpreter, not a listing.

None is generator-numbered. Four of them (`LegacyProductFormatDeclaration`,

`docs/migration-from-upstream.md` is the artifact that exists to account for exactly this,

9.6.1 wheel", §3, §4, §5, the alias tables of §10–§12, with destinations and reasons

  `UpdateMediaBuyErrorResponse`, `UpdateMediaBuySubmittedResponse`, `Refine`,
  `RefinementApplied`

`LegacyProductFormatDeclaration` appears in `MIGRATION_v8_to_v9.md`, but that is upstream's
own inherited migration document, not ours, and it does not say the name is gone.

**This is the finding.** Six of the fifteen are the response aliases of the deleted

**Not checkable here, and I am not passing it off as checked:** whether each of the 836

### The deselect list — every claim re-derived, CONFIRMED

`survey/dropin/deselected-9.6.1.tsv`: 214 rows, **72 distinct node ids** over 26 files, 36
distinct `(model, field, document, pointer, kind)` claims. Denominator column: 71 rows
behavioural, 143 already inside the 104 declared generator-grading files, so the drop from
the behavioural denominator is smaller than the row count suggests and the table says so
itself.

The central claim of every kind is that **the document, read as the conjunction that
reaches that pointer, REQUIRES the field** — which is what makes upstream's default a
laxness rather than a restatement. I re-implemented that independently (own record walk,
own `required` union over `allOf`, own arm scan) and got 29 of 36 agreeing directly. The
7 that did not were **my instrument being narrower than theirs**, confirmed by hand on the
documents:

* `brand/verify-brand-claims-response.json` — I looked for `required` inside `#/oneOf/0`
  and found `['results', 'signed_response']`. The root `allOf[1]` carries
  `required: ['status']`, so the conjunction reaching that arm does require `status`. The
  table is right; my walk did not climb to the enclosing conjunct, which
  `invented_defaults`'s own docstring says is the point of the reading.
* `core/publisher-property-selector.json` — `oneOf[1]` requires `publisher_domain`;
  `oneOf[0]` and `oneOf[2]` declare it without requiring it. "Required by some arm" is true
  at the union; I was looking for arms *inside* an arm.

So: **36 of 36 claims hold.** Spot-checks with document and pointer named, as asked:
`core/protocol-envelope.json#/required` → `['status']`;
`brand/verify-brand-claims-response.json#/allOf/1/required` → `['status']`;
`core/publisher-property-selector.json#/oneOf/1/required` → `['publisher_domain', 'selection_type', 'property_ids']`.

One wrong turn of my own, recorded because it would mislead anyone repeating this: comparing
the table's `upstream_default` column to `json.dumps(field.get_default())` reports 186
mismatches, all spurious — `csv.DictReader` strips the column's quoting and the actual
defaults are `enum` members whose `.value` is the string. The column is correct.

### The scope finding: 41 upstream test files are edited

This is the one place the brief's expectation — "types ADDED, tests REMOVED, and nothing
else" — does not describe the tree. 41 of the 650 surviving files differ from upstream's
bytes: **+568 / −232 lines.**

What the edits are, classified over every changed line:

* **94 added lines supply `status="completed"`** (or the same key in a dict), each with a
  comment of the form *"`status` is required by core/protocol-envelope.json, which this
  document composes via allOf. Upstream's generator dropped allOf-composed required fields,
  so their fixtures omit it."* — fixture ports, and the document they cite is the one I
  verified above.
* 43 import/name lines the ported fixtures need.
* 362 comment lines, almost entirely those explanations.
* **0 assertions added, 0 assertions changed.** 13 assertion lines are removed, and every
  one sits inside a removed test function in the 3 files above, except one in
  `test_native_ci.py` that names the deleted `tests/test_versioned_bases.py`.

So the edits are fixture repairs, not assertion repairs, and the assertion surface of the
kept suite is upstream's unchanged. That is the reassuring half. The finding is the other
half: **`survey/dropin/deselect.py`'s docstring states "UPSTREAM'S SUITE STAYS
BYTE-IDENTICAL. Nothing is edited under `tests/`."** Read as a statement about that
module's own behaviour it is true — the deselect mechanism writes no test. Read as the
statement about the tree that its wording makes, it is false by 41 files, and a reader of
that docstring would not expect to find `status="completed"` added to a fixture. The
sentence should be scoped to the mechanism, or the ports should be named beside it.

## §3. The classification — NOT CONFIRMED (both directions)

`survey/upstream_test_character.py` re-run against `/srv/ws/wt-upbase` reproduces the
quoted figures exactly: **159 DELETE / 370 PORT / 9,565 BEHAVIOUR** functions (10,094
total), **924 / 800 / 19,927** items. The rule is stated in the module's own docstring and
is defensible: DELETE is decided by implementation tokens appearing **inside assertions
only** (a fixture may touch `model_fields` freely), PORT by the route, BEHAVIOUR otherwise;
`model_fields_set` is excluded by name because it mirrors a wire fact.

Sampling 10 DELETE verdicts evenly across the bucket, all 10 are correct by that rule —
`__all__` inventories, `model_fields` membership, `__pydantic_decorators__`,
`adcp.types.domains` layout, `__name__` identity. Sampling 10 BEHAVIOUR verdicts evenly,
all 10 assert on envelopes, dispatch outcomes, parsed responses or raised errors. The rule
holds in the middle of the distribution. The edges are where it does not.

### Direction 1 — BEHAVIOUR counted as DELETE (hiding a real failure)

The decision loop takes `ast.unparse` of the whole `ast.Assert` node — **claim and failure
message together** — and `break`s on the first token hit. So a token that appears only in
the message decides the verdict.

**12 of the 159 DELETE verdicts rest on a token in the message, not the claim.** Of those,
at least three are behavioural tests:

| test | claim | token found in |
|---|---|---|
| `conformance/reporting/test_reporting_production_lease_order.py::test_public_stores_lease_and_configuration_writer_keep_consistent_order` | `assert not any(isinstance(result, BaseException) for result in results)` — a Postgres lease/configuration-writer ordering test with a real pool, a real trigger and a monkeypatched `execute` to observe backend pids | `[type(result).__name__ for result in results]`, the message |
| `conformance/reporting/test_reporting_production_lease_order.py::test_lease_never_waits_for_another_account_after_acquiring_one` | same file, same shape | same |
| `test_import_memory.py::test_import_peak_rss_within_budget` | `assert peak < IMPORT_RSS_BUDGET_MIB` — a budget on the artifact's import cost | `model_rebuild` in the message |

Those are deadlock-ordering and import-cost obligations, not statements about a generator.
They are out of the denominator for the spelling of their failure messages. The other nine
of the twelve I judge correctly classified on intent — `test_public_api_surface_matches_snapshot`
and the `__all__` family really are export inventories even when the token sits in the
message — but they are classified correctly by accident of the same defect.

A second edge, smaller but worth stating: because of the `break`, **133 of the 159 DELETE
functions carry more than one assertion** (629 assertions in total), and the verdict comes
from whichever one is hit first. The most extreme is a 21-assertion function decided by
`assert 'SalesPlatform' in dx.__all__`. The rule is "if any assertion is about the
implementation the test leaves the denominator", which is a defensible reading — but it is
not the reading the docstring describes, and the 629 assertions it discards are not counted
anywhere.

### Direction 2 — DELETE counted as BEHAVIOUR (carrying a test that can never pass)

The token table omits constructs of the same family it already deletes. Scanning only the
**claims** of BEHAVIOUR functions:

* `model_json_schema()` — **7 functions**, e.g.
  `test_targeting_overlay_schema.py::test_advertised_targeting_fields_carry_the_description_the_schema_declares`
  asserting `set(fields) == set(TargetingOverlayInput.model_json_schema()['properties'])`.
  That is a field-inventory assertion, the same thing `model_fields` is deleted for, reached
  through the published schema. (Two of the seven are closer to behaviour — a published
  `required` list is a contract a consumer validates against — so this group needs a
  judgment, not a blanket token.)
* `model_config` — **6 functions**, all in `test_extra_policy.py`, and two of them assert on
  **generated text**: `"model_config = ConfigDict(extra='allow')" in updated`. The table
  already deletes `inspect.getsource` for being the text of a generated module; these are
  the same assertion without the function call.

Credit where due: `59112a626`'s "WHAT IS NOT TOUCHED" section already names
`test_extra_policy.py` with "dropping its 5 generator-text tests leaves 16 green and 1 red",
so the repo knows about that file. It is still counted as BEHAVIOUR by the instrument, which
means the denominator carries tests the repo has already concluded cannot pass.

Both directions are small against 10,094 functions. Both are in the instrument's rule rather
than in its data, so both will reappear on the next bundle.

## §4. The arithmetic — CONFIRMED

Re-derived from the junit file directly, by my own parse, before looking at the classifier's

```
testsuite attrs : {'tests': 21668, 'failures': 1, 'errors': 0, 'skipped': 2848}
testcase count  : 21668
by outcome      : {'passed': 18819, 'skipped': 2848, 'failed': 1}
```

| bucket | items | passed | failed | skipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DELETE | 924 | 908 | 0 | 16 |
| PORT | 800 | 795 | 0 | 5 |
| BEHAVIOUR | 19,927 | 17,116 | 1 | 2,810 |
| UNMAPPED | 17 | 0 | 0 | 17 |
| **total** | **21,668** | **18,819** | **1** | **2,848** |

924 + 800 + 19,927 + 17 = **21,668** ✔  908 + 795 + 17,116 + 0 = **18,819** ✔

not here: the arithmetic is right, and what the *buckets mean* is what §3 disputes — the 924
DELETE items include the two lease-order tests and the import-RSS test, so the figure
"18,819 passed" is correct while "924 of them are about their generator" is ~3 functions
(16 items on this run) too generous.

## What I could not check, and why

* **Semantic equivalence of the 836 renamed classes.** Verified only that the source
  document is emitted in every case. Pairwise identity is `survey/judgment.py`'s job.
* **Whether the 15 undocumented names *should* be restored.** That is an owner's call about
  reading 6, not a verification result. The defect reported is the missing row, not the
  missing name.
* **Upstream's own run.** I audited the junit as given and did not re-run upstream's suite;
  a re-run would cost hours and the file's internal totals are self-consistent.
* **The 104 generator-grading files** (`survey/dropin/upstream-tests-about-their-generator.txt`)
  are declared data, not derived, by that file's own statement. I checked that the count is
  104 of 681 and that the figure is stated with its population; I did not re-judge all 104
  file by file.
